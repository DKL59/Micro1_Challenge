# Improvement Changelog

How the solution evolved, from the simple baseline through to the final result.

**How these are measured.** Three cases. Verdict agreement is checked by
script for any run returning structured output; the other two metrics are my
own judgement against the result files.

- **Verdict agreement (out of 3)** — does the system's overall verdict match
  the verdict I recorded in CASES.md for that case. `check_results.py`
  compares each structured verdict against the `my_verdict` field in
  `cases.json` and prints the count. The baseline returns free text, so its
  verdicts cannot be parsed and were scored by hand.
- **Figures traceable to a source (out of 3)** — for a case to count, every
  figure the system reports must name the source file it came from and quote
  a line that actually appears in that file. A case with one invented number
  scores zero. This measures traceability to the supplied source file, not
  that the quoted line is itself published data. Iteration 1 records where
  those two diverged. A case in which the system reports no figures scores
  trivially, having nothing to invent — Case 2 in Iteration 1 is such a case,
  which is why the failure is recorded alongside the score rather than hidden
  by it.
- **Attribution errors caught (out of 1)** — only Case 3 contains a planted
  attribution error: a correctly quoted sector figure presented as the
  company's own. There is one opportunity to catch it, so the denominator is
  one. Read the Iteration 1 caveat before relying on that run's score.
  Iteration 2 removed the signpost from the source file and re-tested, and
  the score held; Iteration 3 held it again.

Where scoring is my own judgement it is stated as such, and it rests on three
cases. That is enough to show a difference this large and not enough to
estimate its size. I have reported it as a difference, not a percentage.

| Baseline | Single fixed prompt to gemini-3.6-flash with the claim text only — no documents, no market data, no tools. This is what a user gets today by pasting a claim into a chat window. | `results/baseline.json` — verdict agreement 3/3, figures traceable to a source 0/3, attribution errors caught 0/1, mean 15.8s (14.9 / 19.4 / 13.2) and ~1,832 tokens per case | The baseline reasons soundly from general financial principles and reached the correct verdict on all three cases. It caught the par value versus market price conflation unprompted, showed why bonus shares are value-neutral, and rejected "guaranteed income". But it verifies nothing. It guessed a market price of "NPR 600 or more" and a yield of "3% to 5%", and it accepted the claim's 30% dividend figure, which the sources contradict for the year the claim is about — Nabil declared 12.50% in 2081/82, and the 30.00% is its 2078/79 total. The guess was not merely imprecise but wrong in direction: it concluded the yield was "very close to the 4% FD rate" when the real figure, 12.50/540.20 = 2.31%, sits below the 2.75% floor of the current deposit range. It did the same on Case 3, building a value-trap argument on the claim's "below book value" premise when EBL trades at 2.96 times book. The pattern holds across all three cases: the baseline accepts every figure it is given and argues only about what the figures mean. On the challenging case it reached the right verdict for entirely different reasons than mine and stated plainly that the claim "cannot be fact-checked" without the source. Verdict agreement therefore does not distinguish a grounded system from an ungrounded one. Two real gaps: verification and attribution. |
| Iteration 1 — grounding | Gave the agent the company's published figures and the deposit-rate benchmark, and required structured output: a verdict, each assertion marked verified, contradicted or not_found, with the source quote and file for every figure. Model, cases and instruction otherwise identical — agent.py imports MODEL from baseline.py so the two runs cannot drift onto different models. | `results/agent_v1.json` — verdict agreement 3/3 (unchanged), figures traceable to a source 3/3 (up from 0/3), attribution errors caught 1/1 (up from 0/1, but see caveat), mean 16.4s (25.8 / 11.4 / 12.1 — one slow case; median 12.1s, below the baseline's median of 14.9s) and ~5,338 tokens per case | Verdicts did not move, and that is the finding. Both systems answer correctly; only one can show why. The agent found "Cash dividend: 12.50%" and marked the claim's 30% as contradicted, where the baseline had accepted it. It reported the yield as 12.50 / 540.20 = 2.31% and rejected Case 3's "below book value" premise with a price-to-book of 2.96x — both taken from the source rather than guessed, though both were arithmetic already present in the file rather than performed by the agent. Then the caveats, and they are heavy. Only one of the six substantive quotes — "Cash dividend: 12.50%" — is a published figure. The other five are lines I composed, because the source files mix figures with conclusions: a deposit range synthesised from three rate cards, two conclusions about Nabil's yield, the sector-attribution paragraph, and a computed price-to-book. Two of those five are arithmetic the agent could have derived from the published figures itself; three are conclusions it read rather than reached. Case 3 is the worst instance: the agent's attribution finding quotes the sentence "That figure belongs to the sector, not to EBL, and covers a different period from EBL's 2081/82 annual results", which I had written into sources/ebl.md under a heading reading "Sector context — required for the attribution check". I wrote the answer into the file and signposted it. The 1/1 therefore measures retrieval, not detection, and must not be read as evidence that the agent finds an attribution error unaided. Further defects. On Case 1 the fourth assertion pairs the figure "Dividends declined from 48.00% to 12.50%" with the quote "the share carries price risk a deposit does not", which does not contain that figure — the metric as defined does not catch a mismatch between a figure and the quote offered for it. One quote silently drops the source's bold markers, so it is not verbatim and would fail a mechanical check. And Case 2 returned all three assertions as not_found, two with "N/A" as the quote, and cited the heading "Not yet sourced" as though it were a finding. Its summary does state that no dividend was paid in the last two fiscal years, so that evidence was read but never reached the structured output; the 84% profit fall is absent entirely. Cost: 2.9x the baseline's tokens. Iteration 2 exists to answer what this run cannot: strip the conclusions out of the sources, leaving published figures and the raw sector fact, and re-run. If the attribution error still surfaces, the finding is real. If it does not, that is an honest negative result about the limits of grounding alone. |
| Iteration 2 — decontaminating the sources | Removed my own analysis from the source files: conclusions, computed sections, and headings that named the check being performed. Published figures, tables and rate cards remain, each with its URL, plus one neutral coverage line per file stating what the file does and does not contain. Everything removed moved to `notes/`. Model, cases, instruction and prompt structure unchanged — the only variable is what the source files contain. | `results/agent_v2.json` — verdict agreement 2/3 (down from 3/3), figures traceable to a source 3/3, attribution errors caught 1/1 (now unaided), mean 15.3s (15.0 / 14.3 / 16.6) and ~4,805 tokens per case | The attribution finding survived. With the answer removed, the agent caught it anyway and caught more of it: it quoted the raw sector line to contradict "EBL net profit up 32% this quarter", separately quoted "Nabil Bank is reported as leading the sector" to contradict "best in the sector", and derived both supporting figures itself — price to book 729.50 / 246.74 = 2.96, and EBL's own growth (4839903472 - 3703225086) / 3703225086 = 30.69%. Both of those numbers were sitting in the source file in Iteration 1 waiting to be copied. The 1/1 now measures detection rather than retrieval. Quote provenance also changed: where Iteration 1 returned one published figure out of six, this run quotes table rows, rate-card rows and market data lines throughout. The only lines of mine it quotes are the coverage statements, used as intended — on Case 2 it marked the claimed announcement `not_found` because the file states that announcements are not included, which is the reasoning the coverage line exists to enable rather than an answer handed over. The cost was a verdict. Case 1 fell to "partly supported" because the agent marked "Bank FD is giving only 4%" as verified, quoting Nabil's "Above 5 to 10 years \| 4.00%" — one row that matches, from a range running 2.75% to 4.55% on ordinary individual deposits. It verified the number and missed the claim, which is exactly the failure Case 3 was built to test and which my own CASES.md names in one sentence: verifying a number is not the same as verifying a claim. And `computed` came back empty: it never worked out 12.50 / 540.20 = 2.31%, so the comparison that decides Case 1 never happened. In Iteration 1 that yield was handed to it. One assertion verified against two contradicted averaged out to "partly supported". So the metric that fell is the one my source files had been propping up, and the metric that held is the one that now means something. Cheaper too: 10% fewer tokens and slightly faster, because the sources are smaller. One defect persists from Iteration 1 — a figure paired with a quote that does not contain it, here "10.00% to 48.00%" quoted against a line about a 36.97% average. |
| Iteration 3 — teaching it to compare, not match | Two rules added to the agent instruction, aimed squarely at the failure Iteration 2 exposed: where a claim compares against a benchmark that varies by tenure, category or period, do not mark it verified because one row matches — state the full range, name the specific rate being compared against, and say why; and before assessing any claim that turns on a ratio, compute it from the published figures and show the arithmetic. Sources, cases and model unchanged. The only variable is the instruction. Note the difference from Iteration 2: that change repaired a broken measurement, this one changes the system being measured. | `results/agent_v3.json` — verdict agreement 3/3 (restored, and this time earned), figures traceable to a source 3/3, attribution errors caught 1/1, mean 20.3s (24.2 / 18.5 / 18.1) and ~5,620 tokens per case | It worked, and for the right reason. Case 1's `computed` now carries "100 * 0.125 = Rs 12.50" and "12.50 / 540.20 = 0.02314 = 2.31%" — the agent performed the arithmetic that decides the case rather than copying it from the source or skipping it. The deposit assertion reports a range of "2.75% to 5.55%" and the summary names its comparator: the yield sits below individual fixed deposit rates "that range from 2.75% to 5.55% across tenures and categories (including Nabil's 5-to-10 year normal FD rate of 4.00%)". That is precisely the reasoning Iteration 2 could not produce. Across the three agent runs the arc is 3/3 unearned, then 2/3 earned but incomplete, then 3/3 earned. Now the defects. The instruction forbade marking such a claim verified but gave it nowhere to land, and the agent returned `"status": "partly supported"` — a verdict-level value in an assertion field the schema restricts to verified, contradicted or not_found. The ratio rule fired mechanically on Case 2, computing a dividend yield of "0.00 / 308.10 = 0.00 = 0%" for a case that turns on no ratio. Case 2's third assertion marks "Bonus shares provide free shares to investors." contradicted while quoting the row showing no bonus was paid — right verdict, reasoning that does not connect to it. One regression against Iteration 2: on Case 3 the agent no longer derives EBL's own 30.69% growth alongside the sector's 32.33%, so the attribution catch now rests on one figure rather than two. And the figure-quote mismatch first noted in Iteration 1 survives here too: Case 1's third assertion reports three dividend figures — "12.50% (2081/2082), 10.00% (2080/2081), 11.00% (2079/2080)" — against a quote containing only the first. Both subsequent changes have failed to touch it, which suggests it needs the schema to enforce one quote per figure rather than another rule. Cost: 20.3s and 5,620 tokens per case, up 33% and 17% on Iteration 2. Verification is not free, and the price is now visible in the numbers rather than assumed. |
| Final — where it ended up | No further change. Iteration 3 is the shipped configuration: published-figures-only sources with a coverage line each, my working held separately in `notes/`, and an agent instruction requiring a source quote per figure, a stated range when comparing against a varying benchmark, and computed arithmetic for any ratio the claim turns on. | `results/agent_v3.json` — verdict agreement 3/3, figures traceable to a source 3/3, attribution errors caught 1/1, mean 20.3s and ~5,620 tokens per case against the baseline's 15.8s and ~1,832 | The headline is not the 3/3. The baseline also scored 3/3, on the same three cases, with no documents at all. The difference is that every figure in the final run is offered with a named source file and a quoted line, the attribution error is caught without the answer being planted, and the arithmetic that decides Case 1 is performed rather than assumed. What that cost is three times the tokens and about 30% more time per case. What it bought is an answer a user can check. The honest summary of four runs: verdict agreement never distinguished these systems and was the wrong thing to optimise; traceability and attribution did, and only became meaningful once I stopped writing the answers into the corpus the agent reads. Several defects remain, documented rather than fixed; the Iteration 3 row lists them. The two that matter structurally are that the agent invents an assertion status when a rule forbids one without offering an alternative, and that it still reports several figures against a single quote. Both are mechanically checkable, and the next change is a validator rather than another rule. |
